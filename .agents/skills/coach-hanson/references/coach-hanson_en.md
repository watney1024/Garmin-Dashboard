# Hanson's Marathon Method `hanson`

> ⚠ **Provenance & copyright**: this file is this repo's **summary + application guide**
> of Hanson's Marathon Method (Luke Humphrey / the Hanson brothers), not a copy/excerpt
> of the book.

## 1. Origin & fit

- Source: Luke Humphrey et al., *Hansons Marathon Method*
- Core idea: **"cumulative fatigue"** — no super-long singles (this school's longest
  marathon workouts top out around ~26 km); instead, several weekly load sessions teach
  the body to hold pace while tired; weekend "long runs" are medium-long + quality blended.
- **Good for**: marathoners who dislike 30 km+ singles and prefer steady accumulated
  volume; runners who can guarantee 5–6 runs/week.
- **Not for**: runners limited to 3–4 runs/week (cumulative fatigue needs frequency); those
  who want to train single-long-run tolerance.

## 2. Intensity system & pace source

| type | code | basis | typical session |
|---|---|---|---|
| easy | E | HR/feel, conversational | mid-week `E 40–60 min` |
| Strength run | ST | easy run finishing faster @ half-to-marathon pace | speed-up last `10–15 min` of a long/medium run |
| Tempo | T | threshold (≈ half-marathon pace) | continuous `5–10 km T` |
| Interval | I | ≈10K–5K pace | `6×800–1600` (1–2 min rest) |
| Long run | LR | E/ST mix | weekend `medium-long 13–26 km incl. faster finish` |

- Pace source: E/T/I from the VDOT table (docs/09); ST from "half-to-marathon band" or
  perceived effort.

## 3. Weekly skeleton construction

1. This school needs **≥5 runs/week**; if the profile has `runs_per_week <5`, confirm with
   the runner whether to stay (otherwise suggest 80/20 or VDOT instead).
2. Two "sessions of substance" per week: one **T or I** (Tuesday) and one **long run with a
   faster finish** (Sunday).
3. The rest are E/recovery runs placed between the quality days; **≥48 h between quality
   days**.
4. The long run's share of weekly volume is **clearly below traditional plans** (≈20–30%);
   load comes from total volume & frequency, not one single.
5. Volume +10–15%/week max, obeying the generic safety layer.

## 4. Periodization & volume ramp

- Reference structure: build 8–10 weeks (E + progressive long runs) → core training
  10–12 weeks (quality sessions join; long runs reach ~26 km) → **3-week taper** (volume
  down, frequency and a little intensity kept).
- Cumulative fatigue is a **feature, not an accident**: running a long workout on legs that
  are not fully recovered is expected in the core phase; but injury signals ≥5 or a yellow
  light still trigger the docs/03 actions.

## 5. Tests & gates

- Use non-A races in the core phase as long-run substitutes (the effort stays inside the
  training load).
- This package's gate: mid-core, can the ~26 km long run's faster finish be held? If yes
  the goal stands; if no, race-day starts conservative (consistent with docs/06).

## 6. Red lines & cautions

- **Don't quietly add super-long singles** (>30 km): it breaks the fatigue model.
- Cumulative fatigue ≠ ignoring red lines: injury & yellow/red rules still apply.
- With insufficient frequency (<5/week) this school fails — recommend a different package.

## 7. Strength / auxiliary advice

- Strength 1–2×/week; place it **after quality days or on separated days** so it never
  precedes a quality session.

## 8. Worked example

A 5-run/week runner: `Tue T 6 km` · `Wed E recovery` · `Thu E` · `Sat E short` ·
`Sun Long 20 km (last 10 min ST)`.

## 9. Mandatory checks (post-generation self-check)

1. **Intensity composition**: exactly 2 SOS per week (one T or I + one long run with a
   faster finish), everything else E/recovery; ≥48 h between SOS. Adding a third substantive
   session is a violation — remove the addition first.
2. **Fatigue quantification & management**: quantify cumulative fatigue via weekly volume +
   SOS completion — legs not fully recovered is the expected state; but unilateral injury
   signals ≥5 or a yellow light still trigger docs/03, and privately adding >30 km singles
   to "buy endurance" is forbidden.
3. **Micro/macro cycles**: micro = Tuesday SOS + Sunday SOS with E in between; macro =
   build 8–10 weeks → core 10–12 weeks → 3-week taper; fatigue accumulates via frequency,
   so there is no traditional recovery week — E runs self-regulate instead.
4. **Easy-run doctrine**: E/recovery sessions are judged by **HR and time**; running slow
   while fatigued is a feature, not a regression — never speed up to "not look slow".
